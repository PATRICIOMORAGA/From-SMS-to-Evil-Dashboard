# 5. What victims said

Reviewed threads use only the panel’s internal user id. Excerpts are shortened. This section follows **chat timestamps**. A slide deck that reorders lines for effect is not a transcript.

## The script

1. A withdrawal “failed” or is “under review.”
2. A bank, not the operator, supposedly demands about **10%** “income tax” (sometimes 5%) before the payout.
3. Or the payout account is “wrong,” and a further fixed top-up (often on the order of **CLP 50,000**, sometimes a smaller “verification” amount) will “correct” it.
4. If the victim has already paid a lot, the operator assigns **tasks**.
5. Each payment creates a new excuse. The withdrawal does not arrive.

## Threads

**User 900274** (3 top-ups, about CLP 6.6 million), 19 August, evening. They say the withdrawal is not showing. The operator replies that the bank reported an anomaly and that 10% personal income tax must be paid before withdrawal. The user answers that they will not deposit again: they fell straight into the scam. A further operator message insisting on the tax is timestamped **after** that refusal, not before.

**User 725434** (4 top-ups, about CLP 6.1 million), 11–19 August. The thread is days long. The user asks whether it is a scam **before** the full “10% anomaly” paragraph in that day’s sequence, and later says they do not have the money for the tax and demands the funds back. Days later the operator claims a transfer failed and the account must be corrected. Collapsing this into one screen reverses the order.

**User 869331** (4 top-ups, about CLP 4.6 million), 19 August. The user threatens to post screenshots, then calls the operators thieves, and says they will send evidence to an app store. The operator’s answer is another top-up (about CLP 50,000) to change the withdrawal account so the bank will pay.

**User 826038** (about CLP 7.0 million) says they have already deposited and asks whether they are about to be scammed. **User 889768** (about CLP 12.1 million) describes the bank story and the extra deposits as something the operators invent, and asks for the money back. **User 881495** is the largest in the sample (5 top-ups, about CLP 13.6 million) and also cannot withdraw.

## Sample, not a census

The 25 threads are a **purposive sample, not a random one**: starting from one page of visitor records (500), we followed the threads with recorded activity and deposits first. That over-represents paying victims, so the per-user amounts and the 16-of-25 top-up ratio describe engaged users, not a panel-wide average. Do not multiply these totals out to all ~5,900 visitors.

| Measure | Value |
|---------|--------|
| Visitors on the panel | ~5,900 |
| Messages | ~57,700 |
| Conversations read | 25 (~0.42%) |
| Of those, with a top-up | 16 |
| Sum of those top-ups | ~CLP 89.7 million (~USD 94,000) |
| Users who directly call it a scam or call the operators thieves | 5 in the structured review |

Sixteen of the 25 reviewed users had at least one recorded top-up. “Balance” inside the game is not money received. No card data appeared in these threads. Peso amounts are converted at ~950 CLP/USD for the window; USD figures are rounded estimates.
